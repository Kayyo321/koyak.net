# Night Press — an algorithmic philosophy

*Prototype only. This folder is a lab; nothing here is loaded by the site.*

Night Press believes an image is a negotiation between tone and a grid. Light is not
painted; it is *earned* by dots that swell on a rotated lattice until they touch,
merge, and become paper. The algorithm reads luminance through a carefully tuned
gamma curve, then asks each cell of a lattice rotated off-axis how much ink it may
spend. The answer is a disc radius. The result must look like the product of deep
computational expertise: a meticulously crafted screen whose angle, pitch, and
tone curve were adjusted until the face reads at a glance and dissolves on approach.

Imperfection is seeded, never random in the careless sense. Each cell carries a
deterministic hash from the seed: a whisper of positional jitter (misregistration),
a slight radius wobble (ink spread), and a probability of dropout in the deepest
shadows so that black is never flat. The same seed always prints the same plate —
the reproducibility of a master printer who can pull identical proofs years apart.

Tone is posterized into a few thresholds before it becomes dot size, the way a
photobashed print collapses a photograph into a handful of decisive values. A
threshold slider moves the boundary where shadow gives way to midtone; fewer levels
produce a poster, more produce a photograph. Every threshold was placed with
painstaking attention so the eye and the mouth survive at the coarsest pitch.

Crops are the composition's argument. Seeded rectangles slice the plate and
re-print what lies beneath at a different pitch and a small offset, so the subject
appears to be assembled from several proofs that were never quite aligned. Their
count, size, and displacement are parameters; their placement is a hash. This is
master-level implementation of an old trick: the collage made computational.

The final algorithm must feel finished, not exploratory — balanced, legible, with
the subject's identity intact at every seed. It is a press that runs at night,
pulling ultramarine proofs onto a dark sheet, and every parameter has been refined
through countless iterations until the dots feel inevitable.
